Micro-Turbojet Test Rig
Overview

Testing jet fuels in a small engine.

I commissioned a JetCat P200-SX micro-turbojet test rig at UBC’s Clean Energy Research Centre. A small engine is cheap to run, and I can test fuels back to back under the same conditions. At maximum thrust, its combustor-inlet temperature and pressure are close to those of a V2500 turbofan at idle.

I measure thrust, fuel flow, temperatures, pressures, gas emissions and particle size distributions. Our partners at TU Braunschweig use these data to calibrate and test a chemical reactor network model of the combustor.

Graduate research · experiments

Engine experiments and fuel-dependent emissions

I plan and run back-to-back fuel tests on the P200-SX, and I measure its performance and gas emissions.

In the GPPS campaign at maximum thrust, FT-SPK lowered the measured CO₂ emission index by 1.7% and the NOₓ emission index by 4%.

Graduate research · modeling

Physics-based transient engine modeling

I built a ten-state engine model in MATLAB and Simulink using component physics and models of the rig sensors.

On 9 held-out Jet A dwells, it predicts shaft speed from the measured fuel flow within 0.61% RMS and thrust within 2.0%.

Graduate research · aerosol

Aerosol measurement and sampling corrections

I correct the measured soot size distributions, bin by bin, for particle losses in the sampling lines and the catalytic stripper.

The sampling lines and stripper together pass less than 40% of 10 nm particles and almost none near 3 nm.

Graduate research · facility

Building and instrumenting the test facility

I decommissioned the legacy SCRE research-engine facility, set up the test cell, and commissioned the P200-SX rig and its emission lines. I am now instrumenting the P220-RXi.

On our stand, the P200-SX reached about 200 N at 112,000 rpm, against a manufacturer rating of 230 N. My Station 4 sampling-probe designs for the P220-RXi went out for fabrication.

Research interests

From combustion to turbine flow and contrail formation

I want to understand how changes in jet fuel affect the flow and particles after combustion. One direction follows changes in mixing and heat release through the combustor to the turbine inlet. The other asks how sustainable fuels affect exhaust particles in the young plume, where some become contrail ice. In both, I would use measurements to constrain the models.
