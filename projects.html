Graduate research

JET-CALF / Boeing–NSERC Micro-Turbojet Research

JET-CALF (Jet Contrails from Aviation Lubricants and Fuels) is a multi-institution program on how fuels and lubricants affect aircraft emissions and contrails. I lead the micro-turbojet performance and gaseous-emissions task, and I coordinate the UBC experimental work for JET-CALF, SCALE-SAF and VLAME. Back-to-back tests on a JetCat P200-SX compare fuel use, gaseous emissions and particles for Jet A-1 and FT-SPK. Each result below belongs to one campaign and one engine, so it is a fuel comparison on this rig, not a full-scale result.

Graduate research

CRN Validation & Full-Scale Extrapolation

Daniel Lieder (TU Braunschweig) built a chemical reactor network (CRN) for the RQL combustor of the P200-SX. My measurements calibrate and test it. Against the Jet A-1 data of the ASME paper, it reproduces outlet temperature, EI CO₂ and EI CO within 4.5%. It overpredicts NOₓ by about 1.8×. In the GPPS fuel comparison, it predicts the measured fuel-induced changes in \(T_{t4}\), CO₂ and NOₓ within 1 percentage point. It does not predict the particle-number change.

Graduate research

Fuel-Dependent nvPM & Aerosol Characterization

Fuel effects on soot are a large part of the non-CO₂ question, and the micro-turbojet makes back-to-back particle tests affordable. I integrated an SMPS behind a catalytic stripper and built the size-dependent loss correction for the sampling line and stripper. At maximum thrust, the first distribution had a geometric mean diameter of 8.6 nm, similar in shape to a CFM56-7B at ground idle. In a later back-to-back test, FT-SPK cut the unit-density soot mass by 72%. The particle-number result is less certain, because it depends on the smallest, most strongly corrected bins.

Graduate research · side project

A Physics-Based Transient Micro-Turbojet Model

I built a physics-based transient model of the P200-SX core with a P220 nozzle, in MATLAB and Simulink. It uses component physics, not scaled generic maps. Driven by the measured fuel flow, it predicts speed, thrust and several sensor readings. It also gives an estimate of the air mass flow, which the rig does not measure. That estimate is model-dependent and has no direct validation.

Apparatus design

P220-RXi Station 4 Sampling Apparatus

This apparatus adds a Station 4 sampling port to the P220-RXi casing, with a coaxial dilution probe that samples gas ahead of the turbine. The main purpose is to separate fuel-derived pollutants from lubrication-oil contributions, which exhaust measurements cannot isolate. I designed the probe with a coupled one-dimensional model, drew the probe and installation packages, and sent the designs out for fabrication of parts. No sample has been taken with the probe, so an oil-free sample is a design objective, not a demonstrated result.

Turbulence and reacting flow studies

Turbulence and combustion have always pulled me in. Graduate courses in both areas made the ideas stick, so I kept going on my own time. These are those personal projects.

Independent computational study

Sandia Flame D: What a Reactor Network Discards

A personal learning project on a simple question: how much of a turbulent flame survives when you reduce it to a few chemical reactors? I set it up as four steps, each with a check before the next. I built the CFD, built reactor networks from it, measured exactly what the reduction throws away, and tested ways to put it back. The networks are scored against my own parent CFD. Only the CFD and the tabulated chemistry are scored against the experiment.

Independent computational study

Apollo Ascent Engine Nozzle: A Staged-Fidelity CFD Hierarchy

A personal project to build rocket-nozzle CFD from the ground up. The test bed is the chamber and bell nozzle of the Apollo Lunar Module ascent engine. Each simulation adds exactly one piece of physics to the last. The sequence is 1-D theory, inviscid flow, a thermally perfect gas, viscous flow, turbulent flow and finally the plume. This shows me what each layer of fidelity actually buys. It also shows, very visually, how a nozzle designed for vacuum over-expands and separates against sea-level back pressure.

Independent computational study

What the Grid Can’t See: Recovering Unresolved Turbulence

Every practical turbulence simulation discards the small scales and then has to guess what they were doing. This personal project asks a sharper question. If you watch the large scales for a while, how much can you actually say about the small ones, and how confident can you be? The study has three parts: my own RANS and LES runs, an in-house turbulence solver, and information-based measures of what coarse data determines. I scored my runs against published DNS, experiment and LES data.

A course project in turbulent shear flows. Lubrication-oil-derived material in the P200-SX exhaust complicates the emissions measurements. Prototype software

Combustor Probe Model: Coupled 1-D Design Software

A steady one-dimensional thermofluid application for designing coaxial dilution sampling probes. It takes a probe geometry, an engine state and a commanded dilution flow. It solves the capture, pressure, mixing and heat-transfer problems together. It returns the sample flow, dilution ratio, supply pressure, outlet temperature and a solution class. I used it to design the probe on the Station 4 sampling apparatus page. It is verified against an independent implementation, but it has no experimental validation.

Prototype software

CFD Agents

An experimental, typed Python framework for guarded agentic workflows around OpenFOAM. Language models can interpret a request, explain a failure or propose a next step. Deterministic code, explicit contracts and human approval control what actually runs. Execution success does not imply numerical success, and numerical success does not imply physical validation.

AetherCFD Studio

A Windows desktop workbench that drives OpenFOAM in WSL without hiding it. Every action maps to real dictionary files and real commands, edited losslessly, and every change goes into a revertable command journal. It adds mesh-quality tools, Cantera-based combustion pre-calculations, GPU visualization and data-driven analysis. An AI copilot can act only through steps that the user approves.

Numerical learning exercise

Euler-JEPA mini

A small, deliberately honest method study. Can a JEPA-style latent objective learn a useful correction to a calibrated physics forecast of rotor dynamics? The truth model comes from Euler work and an angular-momentum balance, so every answer can be checked. A lower latent loss did not guarantee a lower trajectory error, correct ODE residuals or preserved control sensitivity.

Prototype software

BOScope: Background-Oriented Schlieren on a Phone

An iPhone and Mac app that I built for fun. It turns a phone camera into a background-oriented schlieren (BOS) system. The app generates a random-dot background and captures a reference frame and a flow frame through it. It then cross-correlates the two frames to find the apparent dot displacement that density gradients cause. Examples are the plume above a candle or a heat gun.
